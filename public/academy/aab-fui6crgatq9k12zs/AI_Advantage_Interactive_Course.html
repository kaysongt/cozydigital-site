Module 1

AI Foundations (Without the Hype)

⏱ About 30 minutes Goal: pick one tool and set it up properly

1.1 What generative AI actually is

The AI everyone is talking about (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) is a category of software trained on enormous amounts of text. You type instructions in plain English, and it produces writing, answers, summaries, plans, and ideas in response. No code, no setup, just a conversation.

The mental model that makes everything click
You have just hired an extremely fast, endlessly patient junior employee with an excellent vocabulary and zero knowledge of your business. It can produce a solid first draft of almost anything in seconds, but only if you brief it well, and it needs you to check its work. Everything in this course flows from that picture.

1.2 The main tools and what they cost

ToolBest known for
ChatGPT (OpenAI)The best known. Strong all-rounder with voice mode and image generation.
Claude (Anthropic)Excellent writer, strong with long documents, follows tone instructions well.
Gemini (Google)Ties into Gmail, Docs, and the rest of Google Workspace.

All of them have free versions. The paid tiers run around $20 per month and are worth it for business use: you get the stronger models, file uploads, and longer conversations. For the price of one lunch a month, this is the cheapest capable employee you will ever hire.

The advice that saves you weeks
Pick one tool, pay for it, and use it every day for 30 days. Tool-hopping is how people stay beginners forever. The skills in this course transfer to any of them, so the choice matters less than the commitment.

Worth knowing about, but not required: Perplexity (AI-powered research and search), Canva's AI features (design), and meeting transcription tools like Otter, Fathom, or the recorder built into Zoom and Google Meet. We will touch these where relevant.

1.3 What AI is genuinely good at

✓ Hand it these

  • Drafting anything written: emails, posts, ads, pages, scripts, proposals, policies
  • Summarizing long things: meetings, documents, email threads, reviews
  • Rewriting for tone, length, or reading level
  • Brainstorming options when you are staring at a blank page
  • Extracting and organizing information from messy text
  • Answering questions about material you give it
  • Turning a rambling explanation into a clean checklist, plan, or procedure

✗ Where people get burned

  • Knowing your business. It knows nothing about your services, prices, or customers until you tell it. Every bad output you have ever seen started with a bad brief.
  • Facts. AI sometimes states wrong information with complete confidence ("hallucination"), and it never fully goes away. Verify names, numbers, prices, claims, and anything medical or legal.
  • Current events. Unless the tool searches the web (many now can), its knowledge has a cutoff date.
  • Judgment. It does not know your relationships, your risk tolerance, or your reputation. Those calls stay with you.

1.4 The one rule

AI drafts. You decide.

Treat every output as a first draft from a talented new hire on day one. You would never let a day-one employee send something to a customer unreviewed. Same rule here, forever. Businesses that get embarrassed by AI are businesses that skipped the review step, not businesses that used AI.

Add a front desk employee saving three hours a week and the number grows. This course pays for itself the first week you use it, and that is the standard every task should meet: hours saved you can actually point to.

2.2 The audit

Open the and list every recurring task you and your team touch in a typical week. Do not filter yet, just list: answering the same inquiry emails, posting on social media, replying to reviews, writing appointment reminders, building quotes, updating the website, chasing invoices, onboarding a new hire, writing up meeting notes.

Then mark each task against three questions:

  1. Is it repetitive? (You do roughly the same thing each time.)
  2. Does it mostly involve reading or writing?
  3. Could it be done without you physically present?

Tasks that get three yeses are your AI candidates. Score each candidate 1 to 5 on time saved per week and 1 to 5 on how easy it would be to hand to AI, multiply the two numbers, and sort. Your top three scores are where you start. That is the whole method, and it is more than most businesses ever do. The audit tool in this course does the scoring and ranking for you.

Module 3

Prompting That Actually Works

⏱ About 60 minutes Goal: the briefing skill + your two multiplier assets Workbook: Brand Voice Builder, Prompt Library

3.1 Why the same tool gives one business gold and another business garbage

The difference is never the tool. It is the brief. A vague request gets a generic answer; a specific brief gets work you can use. Prompting is not a technical skill, it is a delegation skill, and if you have ever trained an employee you already have it.

3.2 The five-part prompt formula

  1. Role: who the AI should be. "You are a social media copywriter for a med spa."
  2. Context: your business, audience, and goal. This is where most people give too little.
  3. Task: exactly what you want, including quantity. "Write 3 caption options."
  4. Format: length, structure, and tone. "Under 80 words, warm and confident, end with a booking call to action."
  5. Example: paste something you like. One good example beats three paragraphs of description.

See the difference in practice

✗ Weak prompt

Write an Instagram post about Botox.

Gets you something that could be for any med spa in America.

✓ Strong prompt

You are a social media copywriter for a med spa in Sacramento.
Our audience is women 30 to 55, professional, wellness-focused.
Write 3 Instagram caption options announcing 20% off Botox
consultations for new patients in July. Tone: warm and confident,
no pushy sales language, no medical claims or guaranteed results.
Under 80 words each. End each with a booking call to action.
Here is a past caption our audience loved: [paste it]

Gets you three options you can lightly edit and schedule. Same tool, thirty extra seconds of briefing.

3.3 Iterate like a conversation, not a slot machine

The first output is a starting point. Push back the way you would with a person: "shorter," "less formal," "give me five more options," "combine the opening of option 2 with the ending of option 4," "now write the same thing for email." Two or three rounds of direction usually gets you from decent to done.

Reality check
People who accept the first output and complain that AI is mediocre are grading a first draft as a final.

3.4 Your brand voice document, the highest-leverage asset in this course

A brand voice document is one page that describes your business, who you serve, how you sound, phrases you use, phrases you never use, your standing offers, and a sample of writing you like. You create it once, then paste it at the start of any marketing or communication conversation with AI. From that moment, everything it writes sounds like you.

This one page is the difference between AI content that reads like a robot wrote it and AI content your regulars cannot tell apart from your own writing. It takes about 20 minutes in the , and once you build it there, this course will automatically attach it to every prompt you copy from the 25-prompt library. Do not skip it.

3.5 Build a prompt library

When a prompt produces something great, do not let it evaporate. Save it in a shared document with a clear name: "Monthly content batch," "Negative review reply," "Quote follow-up." Over a few weeks you build a library your whole team can run without you, which is the moment AI stops being your hobby and becomes a business system. The seeds it.

3.6 Quality control

  • Verify every fact, number, price, name, and claim before anything is published or sent.
  • Read customer-facing text out loud once. Robotic phrasing is easier to hear than to see.
  • Anything medical, legal, or financial gets reviewed by a qualified person before it goes out. No exceptions.

Module 4

AI for Marketing and Content

⏱ About 60 min setup, then ~2 hrs/month Goal: a month of marketing in a couple of hours Workbook: prompts 1 to 8

4.1 A month of social content in one sitting

Consistency beats brilliance on social media, and AI makes consistency cheap. Here is the workflow, start to finish:

1
Brainstorm. Paste your brand voice document and ask for 30 post ideas for your audience, mixing education, promotion, social proof, and personality. (Prompt 1 in the library.)
2
Select. Pick the 12 to 16 ideas that fit your month. You are the editor; this step is where your judgment enters.
3
Draft. Have AI write captions for your selected ideas in batches, in your voice, with your calls to action. (Prompt 2.)
4
Edit. Tighten, fact-check, and add the specific details only you know. This is the 20% that makes it yours.
5
Visuals. Pair captions with real photos of your business, your team, and your work. Real photos outperform stock and AI images for local businesses. Canva covers the design layer.
6
Schedule. Load everything into your scheduler (Meta Business Suite is free) and be done with social media for the month.
The compounding effect
First month this takes three hours. By month three it takes ninety minutes, because your prompt library and brand voice document are doing the heavy lifting.

4.2 Email: the channel small businesses underuse

Your customer list is the marketing asset you fully own, and AI removes the main reason owners neglect it: writing takes too long. Use it for:

  • Monthly newsletters (Prompt 3)
  • Promotions (Prompt 5)
  • Welcome sequences for new customers
  • Reactivation campaigns: "we have not seen you in six months, here is a reason to come back" (Prompt 4)

Reactivation emails in particular are close to free money for appointment-based businesses.

4.3 Blogs, SEO, and getting recommended by AI

AI is excellent at outlining and drafting the service pages and articles that bring in search traffic. The catch: search engines and readers both reward content with real expertise and local specifics, so your edit pass must add the things AI cannot know, your actual process, your real prices or ranges, your city, your answers to the questions customers ask you in person. Publish generic AI text and you will blend into the same beige paste as every competitor who did the same thing.

The shift worth knowing: GEO
Your next customer may not Google you at all. More people now ask ChatGPT or similar tools for recommendations ("best med spa near me," "dentist in Sacramento that does same-day crowns"). Getting mentioned by AI tools rewards the same fundamentals: clear service pages, consistent business information everywhere online, strong reviews, and genuinely useful content. This is sometimes called GEO (generative engine optimization), and doing the work in this module puts you ahead of most local competitors on it.

4.4 Ads: use AI as a variation machine

Winning ads come from testing, and testing needs volume. Ask AI for ten headline options and five primary-text options from one offer (Prompt 6), pick the strongest few, and test. What used to be an afternoon of copywriting becomes ten minutes of selecting.

4.5 The repurposing system

Never create one piece of content when you can create one asset. Every blog post or long piece becomes a family of content with one prompt:

The repurposer
Here is a blog post from my business: [paste post]
Repurpose it into:
1. Four short social captions, each with a different hook
2. One email to my list summarizing the key point
3. One FAQ answer for my website
4. One 30-second video script I can film on my phone
Match the voice and rules in my brand voice document above.

4.6 Mistakes that make AI marketing backfire

  • Publishing unedited AI text. Audiences have learned the smell of it, and it quietly erodes trust.
  • Letting every post sound the same. Vary hooks and formats; your brand voice document prevents drift, your editing prevents monotony.
  • Making claims you cannot back up. AI will happily write "guaranteed results." You know better.
  • Ignoring your industry's advertising rules. Aesthetics, healthcare, finance, and legal all have lines (before-and-after claims, testimonials, guarantees). Know yours; when in doubt, have the qualified person on your team review.

Module 5

AI for Customer Communication and Sales

⏱ About 60 minutes Goal: answer faster, follow up consistently, never lose a lead to a slow reply Workbook: prompts 9 to 19, review templates

5.1 Reviews: respond to every single one

Review responses are marketing. Prospects read them to learn how you treat people, and search engines notice engaged profiles. AI removes the excuse for the graveyard of unanswered reviews: paste your brand voice document, paste the review, and ask for a reply in your voice. Thirty seconds per review.

For negative reviews, the formula is: thank them → acknowledge the frustration without arguing the details in public → state your standard → move it offline ("call or email us so we can make this right"). AI drafts it calmer than you feel, which is exactly what you want.

One rule for healthcare businesses
Never confirm that a reviewer is a patient or discuss any details of their visit in a public reply, even if they shared details themselves. Respond generically and take it offline. The healthcare-safe template is in the

5.2 Speed to lead

When someone inquires, they are usually inquiring with two or three of your competitors at the same time. The business that responds first, with a helpful answer, wins a shocking share of those decisions. Speed is a sales strategy dressed up as an admin task.

Build your top-ten inquiry pack: list the ten questions you get most (pricing, availability, what to expect, insurance, parking, whatever your reality is), have AI draft a warm, complete reply to each in your voice (Prompt 11), edit once, and save them as templates in your email and text tools. Your team now answers in two minutes what used to wait until "after lunch."

A step further: a website chat assistant that answers hours, services, and pricing-range questions around the clock. Plenty of affordable tools do this now; what matters is loading it with your real answers (your inquiry pack) and clearly labeling it as automated, with an easy path to a human.

5.3 Follow-up sequences: where the forgotten money lives

Most small businesses lose more revenue to weak follow-up than to weak advertising. Every one of these is a short message sequence AI can draft in minutes:

MomentSequence
Quote or consultation sent, no responseCheck-in at day 3, value-add at day 7, polite close-the-loop at day 14 (Prompt 12)
No-show or cancellationWarm invite to rebook, one-tap link (Prompt 13)
Post-visitThank you + review request
Lapsed customerReactivation at 3 or 6 months (Prompt 4)

Draft the sequences with AI once, load them into whatever booking, CRM, or email tool you already use, and they run forever.

5.4 Scripts and cheat sheets for your front desk

Your front desk or phone person handles the same hard moments over and over: the price objection, the "I need to think about it," the upset caller. Have AI draft response scripts for your top five hard moments (Prompt 17), refine them with your team's real experience, and print the cheat sheet. New hires sound like seasoned staff in week one.

5.5 The line you do not cross

Draft generic. Personalize in your own systems.
Draft messages generically ("a patient asked about pricing for X," "a customer is unhappy about a delay"), and add names and personal details inside your own email, CRM, or practice management system, not inside the AI tool. Customer lists and patient information do not get pasted into consumer AI tools, period. Module 8 covers exactly why and what your safe options are.

Module 6

AI for Operations and Admin

⏱ About 45 minutes Goal: get the invisible time-sinks off your plate Workbook: prompts 20 to 25

6.1 Meetings that document themselves

Record your meetings (with everyone's knowledge and consent), transcribe them with a tool like Otter, Fathom, or the recorder built into Zoom or Google Meet, and have AI produce a summary with three sections: decisions made, action items with owners, and open questions (Prompt 20). Email it to attendees within the hour. This one habit ends the "wait, what did we decide" problem forever, and it takes two minutes.

6.2 SOPs by talking

Every owner knows they should document how the business runs, and almost nobody does, because writing procedures is miserable. Here is the cheat: explain the task out loud as if training a new hire, using your phone's voice memo or the AI tool's voice mode, then hand the transcript to AI:

SOP from a voice ramble
Below is a transcript of me explaining how we [task] at my business.
Turn it into a clear step-by-step SOP a brand-new employee could
follow without asking questions. Use numbered steps, bold any
warnings or must-not-skip items, and list needed tools or logins
at the top. Flag anything that seems unclear or missing.
Transcript: [paste]

Review it, fix what it got wrong, done. Ten minutes of talking becomes a real procedure. A documented business is easier to delegate, easier to scale, and worth more if you ever sell it. Do one per week and in six months your operations manual exists.

6.3 Hiring and training

Job descriptions, interview question sets, onboarding checklists, and training guides are all first-draftable in minutes (Prompts 22 to 23). Describe the role, your standards, and your culture; edit what comes back. The quality bar for these documents in most small businesses is low enough that AI plus twenty minutes of your editing beats what most competitors are working with.

6.4 Making sense of your numbers

Paste a spreadsheet export (with any sensitive columns like full names or card numbers removed) and ask plain-English questions: What are the trends here? Which service is growing? Which month underperformed and by how much? What should I be worried about? (Prompt 24.)

Explanation: trust. Calculation: verify.
AI is genuinely good at explaining data and spotting patterns. It is less reliable at precise arithmetic, so double-check any specific figure before you make a decision on it.

6.5 Email triage

Three prompts cover most inbox pain:

  • "Summarize this thread and tell me what they are actually asking for."
  • "Draft a polite reply that says X."
  • "Rewrite this so it is half as long and twice as clear."

If your email lives in Gmail or Outlook, the built-in AI (Gemini or Copilot) puts this one click away.

Module 7

Simple Automations (No Coding Required)

⏱ About 60 minutes Goal: work that happens without anyone remembering to do it

7.1 What automation actually means

An automation is just a rule: when a trigger happens, actions follow. When a form is submitted, add the lead to a spreadsheet and notify me. When a meeting ends, save the transcript to a folder. Tools like Zapier and Make let you build these rules by clicking, not coding, and both have free tiers that cover a small business's first automations. Zapier is the easiest place to start.

7.2 Where AI fits in the chain

The magic happens when AI becomes a step in the middle of a rule:

⚡
Trigger: a new lead fills out your website form.
🤖
AI step: summarize the inquiry, flag how urgent it looks, and draft a personalized reply.
👤
Action: put the draft in front of a human for a ten-second approve-and-send.

You have compressed a task from ten minutes to ten seconds without removing the human judgment.

The automation rule

Module 8

Privacy, Compliance, and Your AI Policy

⏱ About 45 minutes Goal: guardrails so your whole team can use AI safely Workbook: Team AI Policy builder
Before we start
This module is education, not legal advice; confirm your specific obligations with a qualified professional.

8.1 The golden rule

If you would not post it on a public bulletin board,
do not paste it into a consumer AI tool.

That covers customer lists with contact details, anything about a person's health, financial records with account numbers, passwords and logins, and employee records. Consumer AI tools are general-purpose services; treat them like a very smart stranger, because that is what they are.

The good news: almost everything in this course works with generic drafting. "Write a follow-up for a customer whose project was delayed" needs no name attached. Draft generic in the AI tool, personalize inside your own systems. That single habit eliminates most of the risk.

8.2 If you run a healthcare business, read this twice

Med spas, dental offices, clinics, and anyone else handling patient information: patient health information (PHI) is protected under HIPAA, and this is the one area where casual AI use can create real legal exposure. The rules of the road:

  • PHI never goes into a consumer AI tool. Consumer versions of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and similar tools do not offer the Business Associate Agreement (BAA) that HIPAA requires of vendors handling PHI.
  • Names are not the only identifiers. Dates of birth, contact details, photos, and even specific combinations of details (treatment + date + neighborhood) can identify a person. When in doubt, leave it out.
  • Safe uses are everywhere: marketing content, generic patient education materials (with clinical review), SOPs, review replies that never confirm patient status, staff training, and internal documents.
  • If you genuinely need AI to touch PHI (for example, clinical documentation), that requires enterprise-grade tools where a BAA is in place, selected with guidance from a compliance professional. That is a deliberate procurement decision, never a paste into a chat window.

8.3 Settings worth two minutes

In whichever tool you chose, open the data settings and check how your conversations are used. Business and team tiers of the major tools generally commit to not training on your data, one more reason paid tiers are the right call for company use. Turn off anything that shares more than you want, and have your team do the same on their work accounts.

8.4 Honesty rules that protect your reputation

  • Expert-sounding content gets expert review before publishing. Health, legal, and financial advice with your name on it is your liability, not the AI's.
  • Chatbots and automated messages get labeled as automated, with an easy path to a human.
  • Your industry's advertising rules still apply to AI-written ads. The tool will not stop you from writing "guaranteed results." Your policy should.

8.5 Your one-page team AI policy

The moment more than one person at your business uses AI, you need shared rules, because your risk is set by your least careful employee. The generates a fill-in-the-blank policy covering: which tools are approved, what data never goes in, what requires review before it is sent or published, how AI-assisted work gets checked, and who to ask when unsure.

Fill it in, walk the team through it in a 20-minute meeting, have everyone acknowledge it, and revisit it twice a year. That puts you ahead of the vast majority of small businesses.

The 25-Prompt Starter Library

Every prompt assumes your brand voice document is pasted at the top of the conversation. Replace every [bracket]. When a prompt earns its keep, copy it into your own team prompt library and tweak from there.

1 · Content idea generatorMarketing
Generate 30 social media post ideas for my business for [month].
Mix: 40% educational, 25% promotional, 20% social proof and
results, 15% behind-the-scenes personality. Consider anything
seasonal about [month]. Present as a numbered list with a
one-line description of each idea. No captions yet.
2 · Caption batchMarketing
Write captions for these post ideas: [paste your selected ideas].
Rules: under 100 words each, start with a hook line that stops
the scroll, one idea per caption, end with our call to action,
3 to 5 relevant hashtags. Vary the hook style across the batch
(question, bold statement, myth-buster, mini-story).
3 · Email newsletter draftMarketing
Draft our [month] email newsletter. Sections: a 2-sentence
personal opening from the owner, one useful tip our audience
will actually save, one promotion: [offer], one customer
spotlight or result: [paste details]. Under 350 words total.
Subject line: give me 5 options under 45 characters.
4 · Reactivation emailMarketing
Write a win-back email to customers we have not seen in
[6] months. Warm, no guilt-tripping. Remind them what they
loved, mention what is new: [anything new], include this
incentive to return: [offer], end with a one-click booking
call to action. Under 150 words. Give me 2 versions:
one friendly, one short and direct.
5 · Promo announcement, multi-channelMarketing
We are running this promotion: [details, dates, terms].
Create: 1) an Instagram caption, 2) a Facebook post,
3) an email to our list, 4) a text message under 160
characters, 5) a script for a 20-second phone-camera video.
Keep the offer details identical everywhere.
6 · Ad copy variationsMarketing
Write ad copy for this offer: [offer and audience].
Give me 10 headline options under 40 characters and
5 primary text options under 125 words. Vary the angle:
pain point, aspiration, social proof, urgency, curiosity.
No claims we cannot back up, no guaranteed results.
7 · Blog outlineMarketing
Outline a blog post titled '[working title]' for our audience.
Goal: answer the real questions people ask before buying
[service]. Give me an H2/H3 outline, the questions each
section must answer, and where I should add my own expertise,
local details, and real examples that AI cannot know.
8 · Blog repurposerMarketing
Here is a blog post from my business: [paste post].
Repurpose into: 1) four short social captions with different
hooks, 2) one email to my list, 3) one FAQ answer for our
website, 4) one 30-second video script I can film on my phone.
9 · Positive review replyCustomers
Reply to this positive review in our voice: [paste review].
Under 60 words, thank them by first name, reference one
specific thing they mentioned, invite them back. Do not
sound like a template.
10 · Negative review replyCustomers
Reply to this negative review: [paste review]. Calm and
professional: thank them, acknowledge the frustration without
arguing details publicly, state our standard, invite them to
contact [name/phone/email] so we can make it right. Under 80
words. Do not admit fault for specifics we have not verified.
If we are a healthcare business: do not confirm they are a
patient or reference any visit details.
11 · Top-ten inquiry packCustomers
Here are the 10 questions we get asked most, with the raw
answers: [list them]. Write a warm, complete reply template
for each that we can save in our email and text tools.
Each should answer fully, add one helpful extra detail,
and end by inviting them to book or ask more.
12 · Quote follow-up sequenceCustomers
Write a 3-message follow-up sequence for someone who received
a quote/consultation for [service] but has not responded.
Message 1 (day 3): helpful check-in, offer to answer questions.
Message 2 (day 7): add value (a tip, a relevant result we got
for someone similar). Message 3 (day 14): polite close-the-loop
with an easy yes/no. Friendly, zero pressure, each under 100 words.
13 · No-show rebooking messageCustomers
Write 2 versions of a message to someone who missed their
appointment: one text under 160 characters, one short email.
Warm, no shaming, make rebooking one tap: [booking link].
14 · FAQ page copyCustomers
Using our top-ten inquiry pack above, write an FAQ page for
our website. Clear question headings, conversational answers,
2 to 4 sentences each, in our voice. Add 3 more questions
you think buyers of [service] would want answered.
15 · Appointment reminder setCustomers
Write our appointment reminder messages: 1 week before,
1 day before, and 2 hours before. Texts under 160 characters
each. Include what to bring or how to prepare: [details],
and how to reschedule: [method]. Friendly, not robotic.
16 · Proposal draftSales
Draft a proposal for [client name/type] for [service].
Their situation: [notes]. Structure: their goal in their
words, our recommended approach, scope and deliverables,
timeline, investment: [price], next step. One page,
confident, no filler or jargon.
17 · Objection response sheetSales
Our team hears these objections: [list, e.g., 'too expensive,'
'I need to think about it,' 'my cousin does this cheaper'].
For each, write a respectful 2 to 3 sentence response that
acknowledges the concern, reframes the value, and asks one
good follow-up question. Format as a printable cheat sheet.
18 · Upsell and cross-sell ideasSales
Here are our services and prices: [list]. For each service,
suggest natural add-ons or follow-up services, when to offer
them (at booking, at checkout, at follow-up), and a one-line
way to offer each that feels helpful rather than salesy.
19 · Case study from messy notesSales
Turn these rough notes about a customer result into a short
case study: [paste notes]. Structure: the situation, what we
did, the result, one quote we could ask them to approve.
Under 250 words. Do not invent details or numbers; flag
anything missing that I should fill in. Remove or genericize
any identifying details until we have written permission.
20 · Meeting summaryOperations
Summarize this meeting transcript in three sections:
1) Decisions made, 2) Action items with owner and due date,
3) Open questions. Keep it under one page.
Transcript: [paste]
21 · SOP from a voice rambleOperations
Below is a transcript of me explaining how we [task].
Turn it into a step-by-step SOP a brand-new employee could
follow without asking questions. Numbered steps, bold any
warnings or must-not-skip items, list tools and logins
needed at the top, and flag anything unclear or missing.
Transcript: [paste]
22 · Job descriptionOperations
Write a job description for a [role] at my business.
Responsibilities: [list]. Must-haves: [list]. Nice-to-haves:
[list]. Pay range: [range]. Make the opening paragraph sell
why working here is good, in our voice, without corporate
buzzwords. Include a simple 'how to apply' that filters for
people who read the whole post.
23 · Interview question setOperations
Create an interview guide for the [role] above: 8 questions
mixing experience, judgment (give me 2 realistic scenarios
from a business like ours), and values fit. For each question,
add one line on what a strong answer sounds like.
24 · Spreadsheet explainerOperations
Here is data from my business with names and sensitive columns
removed: [paste]. Explain in plain English: the 3 biggest
trends, anything unusual worth investigating, and the one
number you would watch next month. List any calculations you
made so I can verify them.
25 · Weekly report builderOperations
Turn these raw numbers and notes from this week into a short
owner's report: [paste]. Sections: what went well, what needs
attention, decisions needed from me. Ten sentences maximum.

Review Response Templates

Customize once with your business name and voice, then save wherever your team replies to reviews. AI can personalize each reply from these bases (Prompts 9 and 10 in the library).

Positive review: warmPositive
Thank you so much, [first name]! We loved [specific thing they
mentioned], and the whole team will be thrilled to hear this.
We can't wait to see you again.
Positive review: short and personalPositive
[First name], this made our day. Thank you for trusting us
with [service]. See you next time!
Negative review: general businessNegative
Thank you for the feedback, [first name]. This is not the
experience we aim for, and I am sorry it fell short. We would
like to understand what happened and make it right. Please
reach out to [name] at [phone/email] so we can help directly.
Negative review: healthcare-safe (never confirms patient status)HIPAA-aware
Thank you for sharing this. Privacy rules prevent us from
discussing any individual's care or visits here, but we take
all feedback seriously and would welcome the chance to talk
directly. Please contact [name] at [phone/email].
Rules of thumb
Reply to everything within 48 hours · never argue details in public · never copy-paste the identical reply down the page · take real problems offline fast.
